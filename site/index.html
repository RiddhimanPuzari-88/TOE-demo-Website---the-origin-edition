From a Laptop to Paris -- The Story of Studio 11

I've always wanted to build something of my own, maybe because I grew up watching my grandfather, who came to Mumbai from Rajasthan in the 1960s with nothing and built a legacy through grit and generosity. That legacy of creation has always been my quiet inspiration.

My career began at Landor, where I built my foundation -- a firm that works across consulting, design, and experience. I learned discipline, craft, and what it takes to create work that lasts. But after a few incredible years, I found myself wanting to explore a side of design that was more personal, more experimental.

So, I took a leap. I began freelancing, working on projects that pushed me creatively. Around that time, my brother said something simple that changed everything: "Why don't you just start your own studio?"

With just a laptop, a name, and an idea, Studio 11 was born. There was no team, no roadmap, no safety net. Word of mouth brought in early projects, and slowly, the studio began to find its rhythm.

Then came a monumental chapter -- I got married and moved to Paris. Living between Mumbai and Paris opened my eyes to new perspectives, new collaborations, and new creative energy. It inspired me to expand Studio 11 globally.

Of course, the road hasn't been linear. There have been quiet months, steep learning curves, and even a legal battle that taught me the importance of resilience and self-belief. But every challenge shaped the studio and me into something stronger.

The biggest turning point? Realizing that Studio 11 isn't just a business. It's a reflection of who I am: curious, evolving, and deeply in love with what I do.

If there's one thing I'd tell anyone dreaming of starting something: You don't need to have it all figured out. You just need to begin.

I Failed 11 Times Before I Finally Started Building Something That Made Sense

Hi, I'm Sana. Born in Mumbai, raised on vada pav, local trains, and the belief that "agla idea pakka chalega." I always knew I wanted to build something of my own. What exactly? No clue. But that never stopped me.

My founder journey started at 19, when I launched my first "startup": a customised diary business. Turns out... the only person who bought a diary was my mother. Even she asked for a discount. Failure #1.

From there began a glorious series of entrepreneurial experiments that I like to call "11 steps to becoming accidentally wiser."

Over the years, I launched:
A fitness app while never visiting a gym myself.
A home-baked cookie brand even though I burn Maggi.
A matchmaking service for introverts. (They never responded to each other.)
A pet accessory startup despite having no pets.
A fashion label where I forgot to arrange inventory.

And by Failure #7, even my father said, "Beta, LinkedIn pe kuch aur likh de. Founder founder mat likh."

But something remarkable happened with each misstep. I learned. I adapted. I didn't die of embarrassment. And I kept going.

Today, after 11 failed attempts and 11 notebooks full of crossed-out ideas, I am finally building something new... in stealth. For the first time, it feels aligned. Like all those detours were quietly preparing me.

People often ask, "Sana, how do you still have the courage?" Truth is, I don't always. But I have something better: stubborn hope.

I've realised failure isn't the opposite of success. It's the syllabus. And I've just completed 11 chapters.

Don't stop. Don't let one "No" or one bad idea convince you that you're not built for this. You are. You just need to take one more step than the version of you who gave up.

Your failed attempts aren't signs to quit. They're hints that something better is being built inside you. Keep going. We'll get there, one hilarious failure at a time.

-- Sana M.
11-times founder, finally on the right path (I think).
